# Progress notes: placing the bowl on the table

Session: Oct 4 evening to early Oct 5, 2026, on `rchi-cpu-5` (Kinova Gen3 7-DOF arm, RealSense wrist camera).
Previous notes: `PROGRESS_2026-09-29.md`. Next step: `PLAN_2026-10-05.md`.

## Summary
- **Placing works on the real arm, tested with an empty gripper.** `place_bowl.py place --here` levels the hand, lowers it in one smooth move until it feels the table, then opens the gripper and pulls straight back without asking.
- **The arm's base is mounted about 16° tilted.** The code now corrects for that everywhere: level, straight down, and heights above the table.
- **The lab's impedance (compliant) mode isn't safe on this arm.** It dropped the wrist into the table once. Lowering now uses normal position control with touch sensing instead.
- **The table detector ignores the floor**, and picks the right plane when there are objects on the table.

## 1. Table detection fixes (`table_detect.py`)
- **Floor rejection:**
  - a plane farther than the table distance + 0.3 m from the camera is never picked (`MAX_PLANE_DIST`);
  - when the arm pose is known, a plane below `MIN_TABLE_HEIGHT` is never picked either.
- **Bug fixed:** with the "up" direction known, the detector picked the horizontal plane *closest* to the camera. That was the top of the box on the table. It now picks the largest one.
- **A spot search can now be limited** to where the arm can reach (`allowed=...`).

## 2. What the lab already had

## 3. `place_bowl.py` (new)
The commands:
- **`level`:** with the hand held truly level (checked with a phone level) and the camera on the right, it records true "up". The base turned out to be **16° tilted**. Saved to `~/.table_place/mount.json`.
- **`look`:** at the scan pose with an empty gripper, finds the table and a clear, reachable spot (the green circle) and saves it. The camera position comes from the joint angles plus the hand–camera calibration, so no ROS TF is needed.
- **`place`**, starting with the bowl already gripped:
  1. squares the hand: level, camera on the right seen from behind (max 15° turn with a bowl in hand);
  2. carries the bowl over the spot, 30 cm above the table;
  3. makes **one continuous move straight down** at 2 cm/s, at most 40 cm;
  4. stops on contact: the joint torques are read about 50 times a second, compared with the torques from 0.3–0.7 s earlier, and a jump of more than 1.5 Nm for 3 readings in a row counts. It then confirms standing still for about 0.5 s;
  5. opens the gripper and pulls straight back 7.9 cm (2.5 × the lip width) to clear the lip. No prompt.
- **`--here`** skips the spot: it squares the hand and lowers from where it is.
- **`--steps`** uses the older 2 mm step-by-step lowering.
- **Safety checks:**
  - everything is planned with IK before anything moves (`--execute` is needed to move);
  - it requires the "low" speed preset and one ENTER before starting;
  - it compares the arm's reported hand pose against the URDF model;
  - it stops if the bowl isn't touched within the planned depth;
  - it refuses a spot older than 30 min, or one saved before the last `level`.

## 4. What went wrong, and the fixes
- **Impedance mode slammed the wrist into the table.** The lab's compliant controller (`kinova.py`, `fix_joint_hack`) has two problems here:
  - it models J6 as fixed at **−67.6°**, while our J6 was at +60°. I checked against the lab's own "hack" URDF: at +67.6° the controller's idea of the hand position is 53 cm off;
  - it computes gravity as if the base were upright, but it's tilted 16°.

  Its gravity compensation therefore pushed the wrist the wrong way. `--impedance` now refuses unless J6 = −67.6° **and** the base is level, so it's effectively off on this arm.
- **A first-step false contact.** Torques were read too soon after the squaring move. Fixed with a 1 s settle, a measured noise level, and a re-check before stopping.
- **A mid-air stop about 10 cm down.** The gravity load drifts as the arm lowers, and I compared against the start of the move. Fixed by comparing against a rolling reference from just before. Without the fix, a long drop could also have released the bowl mid-air.
- **The back-off turned the hand.** It used the URDF's hand angle. Fixed by using the arm's own reported pose.

## Still open

- **Table tilt:** the Sep 29 frame showed the table within 4° of the arm's level, which doesn't fit a 16° base tilt. Run `look` again after `level` and see what it reports.
- **`look` hasn't been run on the arm since `level`.** Neither has a real placement with the bowl at a `look` spot.

## How to run (on `rchi-cpu-5`)
```bash
# arm stack (terminals 1-3), from ~/feeding-deployment with ARM_RPC_HOST=127.0.0.1:
#   arm_server.py, scripts/stub_base_server.py, scripts/bulldog_bypass.py ; arm_set_speed.py low
cd ~/feeding-deployment-table/table_placement
python3 place_bowl.py level                       # once: hand truly level, camera on the right
python3 scan_pose.py && python3 place_bowl.py look
python3 place_bowl.py place                       # plan only
python3 place_bowl.py place --execute             # or: place --here --execute
```
With the bulldog bypass, the physical e-stop is the only stop.
