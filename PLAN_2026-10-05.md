# Plan for 2026-10-05: scan several spots, average them, confirm by clicking, then place


## Where things stand (end of 2026-10-04)
- **`place_bowl.py place` works on the arm.** It squares the hand to level using the mount-tilt correction from `level`, then makes one smooth move down at 2 cm/s until the joint torques show the bowl touching, and then opens the gripper and backs off 7.9 cm.
- **The spot it uses comes from `look`:** one depth frame, one green circle, saved to `~/.table_place/spot.json`.
- **The problem:** one frame gives one guess. Depth noise, or one bad frame, can move the green circle a few cm or put it somewhere odd, and nothing asks a person before the arm goes there.

## Goal
1. **Scan several times.** Take several frames and find a green circle in each.
2. **Combine them.** Drop the outliers and average the rest into one spot.
3. **Show it and confirm with a click.** Show the result on the camera picture and let the user click **Confirm** (or rescan, or cancel).
4. **Remember it and pass it on.** Keep the confirmed coordinates in a variable, and also in a file, so the placing code gets them.
5. **Place there** with the existing `place` code.

## Design

### 1. Several scans (`look --frames N`, default 10)
- Hold the arm still at the scan pose and grab N depth+colour frames about 0.2 s apart, reusing `grab_frame`.
- **Check for movement:** read the joints before and after. If the arm moved, refuse, as now.
- **Run each frame through the existing detector:** `detect_table(...)` with the same reach/lip settings as today. Each frame gives either a candidate spot (level frame) plus its table height, or no spot.

### 2. Drop outliers and average
- Take the **median** of the candidate spots, in x and y.
- **Outliers:** any candidate more than `max(1.5 cm, 3 × MAD)` from the median, where MAD is the median distance from the median. They're shown in the picture but not used.
- **Average the rest** into the final spot. Table height is the median over all frames.
- **Refuse, and say why, if:**
  - fewer than 3 frames found a spot;
  - fewer than 60 % of the candidates are inliers (the frames disagree; it might be two different open areas);
  - the averaged spot isn't clear in the middle frame's free-space grid. This catches two clusters averaging onto an object.
- Print the spread (standard deviation of the inliers) as a confidence figure.

### 3. Confirm screen (OpenCV window; cv2 4.5.4 with GUI is installed)
- **The picture:** the colour image from the middle frame, with the table in red and objects in yellow, as in `overlay_image`.
- **Circles:**
  - every inlier candidate as a thin grey circle;
  - every outlier as a red X;
  - the **final spot** as a thick green circle, using the bowl's outer radius (bowl + lip, 8.7 cm).
- **Text:** the coordinates (x, y, distance from the arm base), the number of frames used and the spread.
- **Buttons** drawn on the image, clicked with the mouse (keys also work):
  - **Confirm** (Enter): accept;
  - **Rescan** (R): take N new frames;
  - **Cancel** (Esc): save nothing.
- **Projecting circles into the picture:** existing helpers `draw_placement` and `project_to_pixels` in `table_detect.py`, plus the camera pose that `look` already computes.

### 4. Remember the coordinates and pass them on
- **In Python:** a function `choose_spot(frames=10) -> dict | None`. It returns, for example:
  `{"x": 0.851, "y": 0.094, "table_z": 0.132, "frame": "level", "spread_cm": 0.6, "n_used": 9, "confirmed": True, "time": ...}`
  and returns `None` if cancelled.
- **In a file:** the same dict goes into `~/.table_place/spot.json`, same format as now plus `confirmed: true`. So a separate `place` run, or another program, finds it.
- **For placing:** a function `place_at(spot, execute=False)` takes that dict directly. It's the same as `place` with the spot passed in instead of read from the file, so other code can do:
  ```python
  spot = choose_spot()
  if spot:
      place_at(spot, execute=True)
  ```
- `place` refuses a spot that wasn't confirmed, unless `--no-confirm` is given.

### 5. Placing
Unchanged: `place` / `place_at` plan to above the spot, move straight down until contact, release and back off. Reachability is checked by the planner, as now.

## Verification
- **Offline:** the outlier/average helper. Nine candidates within 1 cm plus one 10 cm away gives the average of the nine. Two equal clusters are refused. Fewer than 3 is refused.
- **Offline:** projection round trip. A spot projected into the picture lands where the circle is drawn, within 1 pixel.
- **Offline:** `look --frames` on the saved `robot_depth.png`/`robot_color.png`, repeated with added depth noise. This shows the confirm window without the arm.
- **On the arm:** run `look --frames 10` at the scan pose and check the circles cluster on open table. Confirm, then `place` plan only, then `--execute` with an empty gripper first.

## Open questions to check on the arm
1. **Can the camera see the table with the bowl already in the gripper?** The bowl hangs about 11.5 cm in front of the tool point and may block part of the view. If it does, scan before gripping the bowl, or filter out the bowl's own points the way the gripper's are filtered today (`SELF_RADIUS`).
2. **Re-measure the reach limits.** The limits `look` uses (0.75–0.95 m from the base) came from the old impedance planning. Position-controlled lowering can probably reach a wider ring.
3. **How many frames:** 10 takes about 2 s. Check the spread on the real arm and adjust.
